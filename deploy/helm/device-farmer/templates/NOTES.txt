{{- if lt (int .Values.config.db.maxConns) 7 }}


  config.db.maxConns IS {{ .Values.config.db.maxConns }}, AND THE JOBRUNNER WANTS 7.

  Each jobrunner replica runs 4 jobs at once by default, holds one connection
  permanently for its claim session, and uses one per running job to renew that
  job's lease. Below 7 it logs a warning at startup and then, once the farm is
  busy, quietly stops picking work up: the claim session cannot be built, the
  poll times out, and no error is reported anywhere.

  Jobs already running keep their devices — nothing here ends a lease — but the
  queue stops moving with every component reporting itself healthy.

WHAT THIS DEPLOYMENT PROMISES

  A lease ends when the job says so, when a deadline the user wrote down
  elapses, or when a human takes it back. Nothing else.

  Rolling this release, draining a node, or losing a pod does NOT release a
  lease. farmd drains in-flight work on SIGTERM and leaves the lease alone;
  the replacement re-attaches with the same job_id and gets the same lease,
  the same device and the same fence back. Watch it happen:

    kubectl -n {{ .Release.Namespace }} rollout restart deploy/{{ include "device-farmer.fullname" . }}-jobrunner
    kubectl -n {{ .Release.Namespace }} logs -l app.kubernetes.io/component=jobrunner --tail=50

  `farmd node` is NOT part of this release. It runs on the bare-metal hosts
  because it needs /dev/bus/usb and uhubctl; without it, recovery tiers 3 and
  4 are refused rather than silently skipped. See deploy/helm/README.md.
